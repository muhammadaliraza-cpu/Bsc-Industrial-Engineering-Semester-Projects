# Designing an Optimal Facility for a Local Restaurant

## Project Overview

This academic project was completed as part of the **Industrial Facilities Design** course at the Department of Industrial Engineering, University of Engineering and Technology (UET), Taxila.

The project applied industrial engineering principles to select a suitable restaurant location, develop facility layouts, and optimize the arrangement of functional areas. Multiple decision-making and facility layout techniques were used to improve operational workflow, space utilization, material handling, and customer convenience.

## Project Objectives

- Select a suitable restaurant location using multi-criteria decision-making.
- Develop block and detailed facility layouts.
- Optimize the arrangement of restaurant departments.
- Improve workflow and reduce material handling costs.
- Consider ergonomics, ventilation, lighting, safety, hygiene, and space utilization.

## Methodology

### 1. Location Selection – Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP)

Applied the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) to evaluate alternative restaurant locations using criteria such as:

- Foot traffic and customer proximity
- Rent and lease costs
- Customer demographics
- Parking and accessibility
- Proximity to competitors
- Safety and security

The report's AHP results identified the following location priorities:

| Location | Priority Score |
|---|---:|
| Taxila Chowk | 0.605 |
| Mandi Moor Rawalpindi | 0.291 |
| UET Taxila Gate 2 | 0.105 |

### 2. Facility Layout Design

Developed initial block layouts and a detailed restaurant layout using **Factory CAD**.

The facility included:
- Entry and reception
- Dining area
- Kitchen
- Restrooms
- Storage area
- Waiting area
- Hallways and pathways

The design considered kitchen workflow, equipment placement, customer comfort, employee movement, and functional area relationships.

### 3. Layout Optimization

Applied two facility layout optimization techniques:

**CRAFT Method**
- Evaluated departmental arrangements using material flow and cost matrices.
- Explored department-swapping alternatives.
- Analyzed material handling cost.

**Graph-Based Method**
- Evaluated relationships and adjacency requirements between facility departments.
- Used relationship weights to support layout arrangement decisions.

### 4. Physical Facility Design Considerations

The detailed layout considered:
- Lighting orientation and natural lighting
- Ventilation and airflow
- Ergonomics and workplace comfort
- Space utilization
- Workflow and accessibility
- Safety and emergency pathways
- Hygiene facilities
- Storage and furniture distribution
- Piping and sewage systems
- Aesthetic considerations

## Results and Findings

The project report presented the following findings:

- AHP was used to evaluate and rank alternative restaurant locations.
- Factory CAD supported the development of the detailed facility layout.
- CRAFT and graph-based methods were applied to evaluate and optimize departmental arrangements.
- The report stated a **15% reduction in operational costs** following layout optimization.
- The proposed arrangement emphasized improved workflow, reduced employee walking distances, and more efficient material handling.

*Note: The report contains inconsistent location conclusions: its AHP results identify Taxila Chowk, while its discussion and conclusion identify Mandi Mor Rawalpindi. Both are retained here as reported.*
